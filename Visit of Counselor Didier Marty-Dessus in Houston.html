Dr. Didier MARTY-DESSUS

Dr. Didier MARTY-DESSUS obtained a PhD in 1993 after studies in the field of Physics and Microelectronics in Toulouse, France. He has more than 25 years of experience in the field of science and technology, specifically in electrical engineering and telecommunications, as a teacher and researcher, and for the state administration. During his academic career, Didier Marty-Dessus has published more than 100 scientific publications in peer-reviewed journals with national and international audiences, and was also a co-author of books dedicated to education and learning. Between 2006 and 2010, Didier Marty-Dessus was seconded to the French Ministry of Foreign and European Affairs as Attaché for Science, Technology and Innovation at the Embassy of France in China. He initiated and launched various programs aiming at enhancing research and innovation with different structures in South China, and worked towards developing high-level academic cooperation. In 2010, he held a new position of Scientific Attaché at the Consulate General of France in Vancouver, in charge of bilateral cooperation for West Canada (British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Yukon, Northern Territories). In Feb. 2021, he was seconded once again to the French Ministry of Foreign Affairs and appointed as Counsellor for Science and Technology at the Embassy of France in Japan (Tokyo). He is currently heading the Office of Science and Technology at the Embassy of France to the USA as its Scientific Counsellor since September 2025.

Frank Marchetti

Frank Marchetti has been the consul general of France in Houston since September 2, 2025. He previously served as the deputy chief of mission at the French Embassy in Ottawa. A diplomat since 2007, Frank Marchetti served as desk officer in the Africa and Indian Ocean directorate at the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs; political advisor at the Permanent Mission of France to the United Nations in New York; head of cooperation at the French Embassy in Pretoria, South Africa; seconded to the French Development Agency (AFD) in Paris as deputy Director for Africa; head of the West Africa division in the Africa and Indian Ocean directorate at the Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs; and then Minister-Counsellor at the French Embassy in Ottawa, Canada. Frank Marchetti is a graduate of the École supérieure des sciences économiques et commerciales and the Institut d’études politiques de Paris. He also holds a degree in accounting and finance and is a graduate of the Institut national des langues et civilisations orientales (Swahili studies).

Rice’s Delegation Biographies

Dr. David Sholl

David Sholl is an internationally recognized expert in research strategy, clean energy and scientific leadership. Immediately prior to his position as Executive Vice President for Research at Rice, Sholl served as the executive director and vice provost of the University of Tennessee-Oak Ridge Innovation Institute. A member of the National Academy of Engineering, Sholl has a distinguished record of leadership, including directing the Transformational Decarbonization Initiative at Oak Ridge National Laboratory and advising the U.S. Department of Energy’s Office of Clean Energy Demonstrations.

From 2013 to 2021, Sholl served as chair of the School of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at the Georgia Institute of Technology. He is editor-in-chief of the AIChE Journal, a fellow of the American Institute of Chemical Engineers and the American Association for the Advancement of Science. Sholl’s research has primarily focused on using materials modeling methods to develop new materials and processes for chemical separations and energy applications. He has published more than 400 peer-reviewed articles and has written several books, including “Density Functional Theory: A Practical Introduction” and “Success and Creativity in Scientific Research.”

After earning his undergraduate degree in theoretical physics from the Australian National University, Sholl received his master’s and doctorate in applied mathematics from the University of Colorado, Boulder.

Sholl oversees Rice's Office of Research, which includes the Office of Innovation, Office of Technology Transfer, Office of Sponsored Projects, Office of Research Integrity, the Animal Resources Facility and the Office of Research Security.

Dr. Caroline Levander

Caroline Levander completed her academic training in literature and currently serves as Vice President for Global Strategy at Rice University, where she expands the institution's international impact through new degrees, partnerships, and global centers, including the Rice Global Paris Center and Rice Global India. She also founded Rice Online Learning and co-founded an ed-tech company in 2020 that was acquired by Honor Education in 2022, where she remains a senior advisor. A scholar of transnationalism, race, gender, and higher education innovation, she is the author of several books, including Invent Ed: How An American Tradition of Innovation Can Transform College Today (2025), Hotel Life (2015), and Cradle of Liberty (2006), alongside co-editing numerous volumes and an Oxford University Press book series. She has held prominent board and advisory positions with Coursera, edX/2U, the Fulbright Association, and Venice International University. Her work has been recognized with fellowships from the Fulbright Program, the Mellon Foundation, and the National Endowment for the Humanities, as well as support from the Rockefeller Foundation to lead an international summit on the Future Global University.

Dr. Tanguy Terlier

Tanguy Terlier received his B.S. in physics from the University of Avignon in 2010, followed by an M.S. in nanoscience and an engineering degree from the University of Grenoble Alps in 2012, and a Ph.D. in materials science and surface analysis from Claude Bernard University Lyon in 2015. After completing a postdoctoral appointment at the Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST), he joined Rice University, where he currently serves as a Research Scientist and Principal Investigator in the Shared Equipment Authority, Adjunct Professor of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering, and Lead Project Engineer for the Woodside–Rice Decarbonization Accelerator. An expert in Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry (SIMS), operando characterization, and correlative imaging, he has authored over 100 peer-reviewed publications and holds multiple patents across surface chemistry, energy storage, and materials analysis. Dr. Terlier serves as Chair of the AVS Applied Surface Science Division, represents the U.S. on the ISO Technical Committee for Surface Chemical Analysis, and is a co-founder and Vice President of the North American SIMS Society.

Dr. Junichiro Kono

Junichiro Kono received his B.S. and M.S. degrees in applied physics from the University of Tokyo in 1990 and 1992, respectively, and completed his Ph.D. in physics from the State University of New York at Buffalo in 1995. He was a postdoctoral research associate at the University of California, Santa Barbara, in 1995-1997 and the W. W. Hansen Experimental Physics Laboratory Fellow in the Department of Physics at Stanford University in 1997-2000. He joined the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering of Rice University in 2000 as an Assistant Professor and was promoted to an Associate Professor in 2005 and to a Professor in 2009. He is currently a Professor in the Departments of Electrical & Computer Engineering, Physics & Astronomy, and Materials Science & NanoEngineering at Rice University. Since 2019, he has been Director of the Applied Physics Graduate Program. Professor Kono was a recipient of the National Science Foundation CAREER Award in 2002 and has been a Fellow of the American Physical Society (APS) since 2009, a Fellow of the Optical Society (OSA) since 2015, and a Fellow of SPIE since 2019. Professor Kono is also the founder of the nationally recognized international program for science and engineering undergraduate students, NanoJapan, funded by the U.S. National Science Foundation, receiving the Heiskell Award for Innovation from the Institute of International Education in 2008.

Dr. Peter Hotez

Peter Hotez received his B.A. phi beta kappa from Yale University in 1980, followed by an M.D. from Weill Cornell Medical College and a Ph.D. in biochemistry from The Rockefeller University in 1987. He completed his residency in pediatrics at Massachusetts General Hospital and a fellowship in pediatric infectious diseases at Yale University School of Medicine, where he served on the faculty until 2000. He then chaired the Department of Microbiology, Immunology, and Tropical Medicine at George Washington University before joining Baylor College of Medicine in 2011. He is currently the Founding Dean of the National School of Tropical Medicine at Baylor, Professor of Pediatrics and Molecular Virology & Microbiology, and Co-Director of the Texas Children’s Hospital Center for Vaccine Development. Dr. Hotez is also the Baker Institute Fellow in Disease and Poverty at Rice University. A member of the National Academy of Medicine, he co-developed CORBEVAX, a patent-free COVID-19 vaccine, and was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 2022 for his contributions to global health equity.

Dr. Kirsten Ostherr

Kirsten Ostherr received her Ph.D. in American Studies from Brown University and completed a Master of Public Health (MPH) in Health Promotion and Behavioral Sciences at the UT-Houston School of Public Health with support from an Andrew W. Mellon Foundation New Directions Fellowship. She currently serves as the Gladys Louise Fox Professor of English at Rice University, where she is the founding Director of the Medical Humanities Research Institute, the Medical Humanities Program, and the Medical Futures Lab. A media scholar, health researcher, and technology analyst focusing on digital health ecosystems and human-centered health technology design, she is the author of Medical Visions: Producing the Patient through Film, Television and Imaging Technologies (2013) and Cinematic Prophylaxis (2005). Her work on digital health humanities and global health messaging has been funded by the NLM and NEH, featured in The Lancet, The Washington Post, and NPR, and presented at international forums including the White House, the World Health Organization, and TEDx.

Dr. Matteo Pasquali

Matteo Pasquali received his M.Sci. summa cum laude in chemical engineering from the University of Bologna in 1992, followed by a Ph.D. in chemical engineering and a postdoctoral fellowship in polymer physics at the University of Minnesota in 1999. He joined Rice University in 2000, where he currently serves as the A. J. Hartsook Professor of Chemical & Biomolecular Engineering, Chemistry, and Materials Science & NanoEngineering. He is also the founding Director of the Carbon Hub, an international academic-industrial initiative advancing zero-emission hydrogen and carbon material pathways to decarbonize industry. A pioneer in the liquid-phase processing, rheology, and scalable manufacturing of high-performance carbon nanotube (CNT) fibers, he has co-authored over 230 scientific publications and more than 30 patents. Professor Pasquali is an elected Fellow of the American Physical Society and the American Association for the Advancement of Science, and the recipient of numerous honors, including the NSF CAREER Award, the Kavli Foundation Exploration Award, and the Rice Presidential Mentoring Award.

Rice Best Paper Award, the IEEE ComSoc Advances in Communication Award, and the SIGMOBILE Test of Time Award.

Key Facility Overviews

Rice University

Founded in 1912, Rice University is a private research university located on a 300-acre wooded campus in the heart of Houston, Texas. Consistently ranked among the top 20 universities in the United States by U.S. News & World Report, it was recently named one of the "New Ivies" by Forbes. Furthermore, Rice University is now ranked as the ninth best higher education institution in America (America's Top Colleges) for 2024-2025, having been ranked twenty-second the previous year. Rice is distinguished by its programs of excellence in a variety of fields, including architecture, business, engineering, humanities, music, natural sciences, and social sciences.

Rice is notable for having an excellent student-to-faculty ratio (1 faculty member for every 6 students) and for being highly selective (its 9% freshman acceptance rate is comparable to that of Cornell University). In 2024, the Jesse H. Jones Graduate School of Business was ranked 29th among the best Business Schools in the United States (U.S. News, 2022), renowned for its dual master's degrees offered in partnership with Houston medical schools, the school of engineering, and the school of life sciences. Rice boasts two Nobel laureates: one in chemistry (Robert Curl, co-recipient of the 1996 Nobel Prize) and another in physics (Sir Roger Penrose, co-recipient of the 2020 Nobel Prize). The School of Architecture (which has maintained a base in Paris for over ten years) and the School of Music are also highly renowned. A university at the heart of Houston's innovation hub

Ideally located between the Museum District and the Texas Medical Center, a partner of NASA, and owner of The Ion incubator in the new innovation district it largely helped shape, Rice University is embedded in a dynamic ecosystem—spanning innovation funding to industrial development—across the city's key sectors (energy, space, health). The Rice Alliance program structures ties between startups, research, and major groups such as Chevron or Schlumberger. Rice hosts the top graduate entrepreneurship program (Rice Business – Jones Graduate School of Business), according to The Princeton Review. In Houston, startups raised a record $2.34 billion in 2021 (Houston Exponential Report). Houston ranks 5th among the Top 100 startup ecosystems (2022 Global Startup Ecosystem Report, Startup Genome and Global Entrepreneurship Network).

Ties between the university and NASA have been close for decades: no fewer than fifteen NASA astronauts and administrators were educated at Rice. The Rice Space Institute, created in 2000, offers "a unique collaborative environment to address educational, research, and technological challenges posed by space exploration and study," according to its director David Alexander, originally from Ireland and specializing in solar dynamics. Since 2023, the Rice Space Institute has hosted the consulate's International Technical Expert (ETI) for New Space.

Rice, a university aiming for international expansion

In January 2023, Rice expanded its presence in Europe by inaugurating the Rice Global Paris Center in the Marais district. Serving as the university's first international campus, this center acts as a European hub, facilitating exchanges and collaborations with international partners. It provides dedicated space for student programs, researchers, and international conferences.

Rice has seen a steady increase in its international student population, which currently accounts for 12% of the total student body and represents over 70 countries. The university offers more than 300 study abroad programs, including 148 in Europe, and maintains strong ties with Brazil, Australia, China, Costa Rica, and other nations. President Desroches has expressed keen interest in building further relationships with India.

Department of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering (ChBE)

Rice ChBE leverages its location in Houston—the energy capital of the world—to conduct interdisciplinary research. The department focuses heavily on fundamental and applied sciences spanning energy, sustainability, advanced materials, nanotechnology, and biochemical engineering.

Rice Global

Rice Global is the university's central initiative to expand its international impact and academic reputation through global systems and connections. It manages strategic research alignments and academic mobility networks globally, anchored by international hubs such as the Rice Global Paris Center and structured academic/corporate partnerships through Rice Global India.

Shared Equipment Authority (SEA)

Started in 2001, the Shared Equipment Authority is a university-wide service that provides and maintains state-of-the-art research equipment. It manages over 120 instruments across science and engineering—including electron microscopy, mass spectrometry, and the Rice Nanofabrication Facility, which leverages semiconductor industry tools to enable diverse device fabrication for microelectronics and neuroengineering.

Smalley-Curl Institute (SCI)

Named after Nobel laureates Richard Smalley and Robert Curl, the Smalley-Curl Institute fosters a global community of research in nanoscale science and technology, quantum materials, and quantum information science. It encompasses the Applied Physics Graduate Program, the Rice Quantum Initiative, and several specialized centers driving interdisciplinary scientific discovery.

Humanities Research Center (HRC) & Medical Humanities Research Institute (MHRI)

The HRC is an internationally recognized hub fostering innovative intellectual inquiry in the humanities and arts. Building on this foundation, the new MHRI is a unique interdisciplinary institute dedicated exclusively to advancing translational research on the human experiences of health and illness. It brings humanists, artists, and social scientists together with engineers and clinicians to ensure patients are treated holistically.

Rice Carbon Hub

The Carbon Hub is an ambitious climate change research initiative focused on industrial decarbonization. It aims to accelerate the energy transition by discovering ways to transform hydrocarbons into sustainable materials—to house, move, clothe, power, and feed people—without generating CO2 emissions.

Texas A&M Facilities

Zachry Engineering Education Complex (ZACH)

The Zachry Engineering Education Complex serves as the flagship, state-of-the-art center for undergraduate engineering education at Texas A&M University. Spanning 48,774 square meters, ZACH is the largest academic building on the main campus in College Station and features an active-learning, technology-integrated environment free of traditional lecture halls. It houses active-learning classrooms, multi-disciplinary instructional laboratories, student collaboration spaces, and the SuSu and Mark A. Fischer '72 Engineering Design Center—a dedicated hub where students work on hands-on prototyping and capstone projects. Additionally, ZACH hosts centralized academic advising, career centers, and student organization spaces to foster interdisciplinary team-based learning.

Nuclear Science Center (NSC / NESC)

Operated under the Nuclear Engineering & Science Center (NESC) at Texas A&M, the Nuclear Science Center is centered around a 1-megawatt TRIGA open-pool research reactor. The facility provides critical infrastructure for nuclear materials testing, radioisotope production for medical and industrial applications, neutron activation analysis, thermal hydraulics research, and beam-port experiments. Its open-pool design allows flexible sample positioning for short- or long-term irradiations and pulsing experiments. The center serves as a primary hub for training nuclear engineering students, supporting national laboratory collaborations, and conducting foundational research in nuclear security, radiation effects, and advanced reactor technology.

Texas A&M RELLIS Campus & RAD Lab

The Texas A&M-RELLIS campus is a 13.35-square-kilometer high-tech research, testing, and evaluation ecosystem located near College Station. It is designed to bridge academia, defense agencies, and private industry, providing specialized proving grounds for autonomous ground vehicles, uncrewed aerial systems (UAS), hypersonics testing, advanced manufacturing, and cyber-physical systems. Within this framework, facilities like the Radiation Development and Testing (RAD) infrastructure and associated ranges support radiation hardness testing, electromagnetic spectrum characterization, aerospace material validation, and defense technology prototyping. RELLIS serves as a full-scale testbed enabling rapid iteration—"develop, test, break, fix, and re-test"—to transition emerging technologies into operational deployment.
